# Settlement Batch Rerun Runbook

## When to use this runbook

Use this runbook for settlement batch reruns. It is written for the on-call engineer, who may not be an expert in this system. If you are unsure whether it applies, start an incident and ask for help.

## Prerequisites

Confirm that you have the correct production access before you start. Open an incident or change record, and note the start time. Tell the service desk that work is under way.

## Diagnosis

Validate the input totals against the transaction ledger. Record what you find in the incident timeline, because the post-incident review will rely on it. Compare the current behaviour with the last healthy period on the dashboard.

## Procedure

Re-run the batch from the scheduler with the original business date. Tell the merchant operations team when the file has been delivered. Work through one step at a time, and confirm the result before moving on. Stop and escalate if a step does not behave as described here.

## Verification and escalation

Confirm recovery with the service dashboard and a synthetic transaction. Keep the incident open until the service has been stable for 15 minutes. If recovery is not confirmed within 30 minutes, escalate to the duty manager. Update this runbook if any step for settlement batch reruns was wrong or missing.

## Safety checks

Production changes during an incident carry extra risk, so make one change at a time and note the time of each change in the incident timeline. Do not restart everything at once, because a full restart can turn a partial outage into a complete one. Before any step that affects customers, tell the incident lead what you are about to do and what you expect to see. If this work happens near the end of a settlement window, check with payment operations first, because some actions must wait until the window closes.

## Rollback

Every change made during settlement batch reruns must be reversible. Record the previous configuration before you change it, so that it can be restored quickly. If error rates rise after a change, reverse that change first and investigate afterwards. When a rollback is used, mention it in the post-incident review, because it shows where the procedure or the tooling needs to improve.

## Communication during the procedure

The incident lead owns all communication, so that engineers can focus on recovery. Give the incident lead a short update whenever the situation changes, including what you know, what you are trying and when you expect a result. Do not share customer data or credentials in the incident channel. After recovery, write a short note describing what worked, so that the next engineer handling settlement batch reruns benefits from your experience.
