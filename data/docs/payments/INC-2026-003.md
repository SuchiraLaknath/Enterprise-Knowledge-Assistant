# Real-Time Transfers outage 2026-09-20

## Summary

Real-Time Transfers was degraded for 155 minutes on 2026-09-20. The incident was logged with error code ERR-PAY-4091 and handled as a major incident under the payment incident management policy. This report records what happened, why it happened, and what will change so that it does not happen again.

## Customer impact

Approximately 19944 transactions failed or were delayed during the incident. Customers saw declined or pending payments, and the contact centre received more calls than usual. No customer funds were lost, and affected transactions were reconciled after the service recovered. Merchants were told through the usual partner channel.

## Detection and timeline

The monitoring dashboard showed a sudden drop in successful mutual TLS sessions. The on-call engineer acknowledged the alert within five minutes and opened a major incident. The incident lead joined the bridge and began sending status updates every 30 minutes. The service was declared stable after 15 minutes of normal behaviour on the dashboard.

## Root cause

An mTLS client certificate lapsed at midnight. It had been issued manually, so it was missing from the automated renewal inventory. The review found that the warning signs were visible before customers were affected, but no alert was set up to act on them.

## Resolution

The on-call engineer issued a replacement certificate and reloaded the listeners. The team confirmed recovery with synthetic transactions, and the service desk closed the customer communication once the backlog cleared.

## Follow-up actions

Include the full trust chain, not only the leaf, in expiry monitoring. The action has a named owner and a due date, and it is tracked in the payments reliability review until it is closed.

## Contributing factors

The post-incident review looked beyond the immediate cause of the Real-Time Transfers incident. Monitoring covered the symptoms, but it did not give an early warning. The on-call engineer had to piece together information from several dashboards, which slowed the diagnosis. Ownership of the affected component had changed in the last year, and the handover notes did not mention this risk. None of these factors caused the incident alone, but together they increased the time customers were affected.

## What went well

The alert reached the on-call engineer quickly, and the incident bridge was open within minutes. Clear roles helped: the incident lead handled communication while engineers focused on Real-Time Transfers. Status updates kept the contact centre informed, so advisers could give customers accurate information. The runbook helped the team confirm recovery, and reconciliation after the incident found no lasting financial differences.

## Lessons learned

The review concluded that the team should detect this kind of failure before customers do. Each lesson from the Real-Time Transfers incident was turned into a tracked action rather than a general intention. The team will share this report with other service owners, because similar risks may exist elsewhere. The report will be revisited at the next reliability review, to confirm that the actions have actually reduced the risk.
